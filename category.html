The "1st Bowman" logo designates a prospect's true inaugural licensed trading card, released 1 to 3 years prior to their MLB debut. By the time a player reaches the majors, 1st Bowman print runs are permanently locked, establishing them as the hobby's definitive rookie autograph benchmark.
Topps Chrome cards are printed on high-gloss chromium metallic card stock that travels across mechanical sheet rollers during production. Even sub-microscopic horizontal roller depressions will automatically cap a card's surface subgrade at 9, driving the strict PSA 10 gem rate down below 26% on high-demand base refractors.
Both cards were printed on the legendary High-Series sheet (#311 through #407) and both suffered massive disposal in the 1960 ocean dump. However, card #311 occupied the vulnerable top-left corner position on the uncut 100-card printing sheet, exposing it to severe corner dulling, miscuts, and print track abrasions, making PSA 9 or 10 specimens virtually non-existent.

Basketball Slabs Purchasing & Grading FAQs

"Game-Worn" patches were physically worn by the athlete during an official NBA regular season or playoff game. "Player-Worn" or "Event-Worn" materials were typically slipped on for photoshoot staging. True high-end collectors and institutional funds pay a 70% to 120% valuation premium for certified "Game-Worn" provenance.
Beckett's "Black Label" requires a card to receive a flawless 10.0 across all four subgrades: Centering, Corners, Edges, and Surface. Due to thick chromium card stocks and high-gloss edge chipping in modern basketball sets, fewer than 0.2% of submitted cards achieve this pristine distinction.
Historical precedent shows that when licensing changes occur, premier cards from the prior era (like 2012–2023 Panini Prizm Silvers) become capped finite vintages that do not lose value. Concurrently, new official on-card Topps Chrome releases capture the lion's share of new incoming rookie capital.
